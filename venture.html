[ the story ]

I was seventeen. I designed pieces I wanted to wear and couldn't find, then ended up running the whole thing: sourcing, sampling, launches, community, every DM.

Most of the growth came from students on their own phones. I put together a small roster of creators across ten-ish campuses in the US and Canada, briefed them, watched what actually converted, and kept working with the ones who moved the needle. Paid channels helped at the edges. Trust did the heavier lifting.

Somewhere in there I got tired of tracking creators and ad spend in a spreadsheet and built a small internal tool to onboard them and see performance per drop. That was the first time I noticed I liked building the system more than posting.

Roughly twenty thousand followers, a lot of reach I didn't pay for, and about half a million dollars moved in the first year. More useful than any of that: I got to be wrong in public and learn how customers, cash, and inventory actually behave.

What it taught me

[ three things ]
01

People buy the feeling

If someone they trust wears it first, the piece almost sells itself. Everything else is downstream of that.

02

Inventory is the boss

A young brand doesn't die of bad taste, it dies of bad math. Margins, cash, and reorder timing quietly decide everything.

03

Small moves, kept up

A caption today, a sample tomorrow, a DM the day after. Nothing dramatic. It just doesn't stop.

What it's giving me for investing

[ the stuff that translates ]
unit economics

I've read my own P&L

Margin, cash conversion, reorder timing. When I recast a client's financials now, I know which lines a founder fights for and which ones they've never looked at.

diligence instincts

I know what a founder leaves out

Because I've been the founder. Customer concentration, returns, the "one-time" expenses that happen every year. I go looking for them.

distribution

I know how attention moves

Who posts, why, and what turns a viewer into a buyer. Useful for judging any consumer business's CAC story, and for spotting the ones that are quietly buying their growth.

ops from zero

I make things exist

The spreadsheet, the vendor list, the workflow, the tool. Being the person who sets it up when nothing did yet, which is most of what a junior seat is.

ai-native

I build my own tools

Outreach engines, research agents, KPI pulls, this website. Claude Code and Python to get fifteen hours back and spend them on the thinking part.

shipping in public

I'm okay being wrong out loud

Put it out, watch what happens, fix it. It's how the brand grew and it's how I want to learn the investing side too.

final drop

One last collection with the team.

SEYA's last drop goes live soon. I'm really grateful for everyone who wore it, shared it, and stuck around. It was my first real swing at building something, and it turned out to be the start of everything else.
