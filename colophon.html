How this was made.

A short, honest note on the process, because I think how someone builds a thing says as much as the thing.

Version one, July 2026

The first version of this site was built in Lovable over a weekend. It was fast and it looked good, but I couldn't really see the code, and every change meant describing what I wanted to a chat box and hoping. It also said I was "exploring tech on the growth side," which was true in June and not true by August, after two banking summers changed what I wanted.

Version two, September 2026

This version is plain HTML, CSS, and a little JavaScript. No framework, no site builder, no build step. I rebuilt it with Claude Code as a pair programmer: I'd describe the layout I had in my head, it would write the first pass, and I'd react to it in the browser and push back until it looked like mine. Every file is one I can open and edit.

Where AI helped

  • The boring, precise parts: the CSS grid for the timeline, the dark-mode toggle, the scroll animations, the SVG ellipse that draws itself around my name.
  • Pulling the content from my old site and my resume into one place so I could rewrite it instead of starting from a blank page.
  • Catching the things I'd have missed: the mobile layout, alt text on the photos, making the ticker pause when you hover on it.

What was hard

  • The writing. Every draft the AI produced sounded like a very polished person who was not me. I cut the em-dashes, the tidy triplets, the "genuinely" and "sharper than I expected." I rewrote the "Notes" page almost entirely by hand, because those are things I actually learned and I wanted them in my own words.
  • Deciding what to leave out. I had a lot of bullet points from a resume. A website is not a resume. It should sound like a person.
  • Being honest about the year. My interests changed between June and September and the site had to say so.

How I kept it mine

The index labels, the handwriting, the blue, and the clock in the corner all carried over from version one. Those were my choices in July and I still like them. It went dark because I read everything on a screen at night, but the cream version is one click away. The SEYA photos are from our real shoot. The Spotify playlist is my actual playlist. The "off the clock" list is real: I do make waffles.

The rule I used for every paragraph: if I wouldn't say it out loud to a friend, it doesn't go on the site.
